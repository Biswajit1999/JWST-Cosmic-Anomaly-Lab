I've turned my JWST Cosmic Anomaly Lab project into a research preprint exploring
how public telescope data can help investigate unusual astronomical sources,
including candidates relevant to gas-enshrouded black-hole growth.

The project includes:

- Analysis of 4,731 selected JWST PRISM source records.
- An overlapping census of all 5,190 JADES DR4 target records.
- Actual telescope images, signed spectral measurements, higher-resolution
  follow-up and synthetic controls.
- Additional Hubble/Spitzer photometry and Chandra catalogue context.

One of the most useful results was a failed confirmation: two striking Balmer
line ratios changed substantially between released reductions. That showed why
reproducibility and calibration checks matter before interpreting an anomaly.

This project has not discovered a new black-hole star. It is an open, reproducible
investigation with a documented follow-up queue, and the attached preprint has
not been peer reviewed. The published MoM-BH*-1 discovery behind recent
headlines belongs to Naidu and collaborators; my paper distinguishes their
findings from this project's measurements.

The project uses Python and public astronomical archives. The repository
includes the analysis code, archive receipts and a record of how the work was
prepared. Feedback on extraction effects and line modelling is welcome.
